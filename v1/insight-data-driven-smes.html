63 Million SMEs, and Most Are Flying Blind

NORTHSTAR TEAM · DATA & DECISION-MAKING

India has over 63 million SMEs. Most of them are making their most important business decisions without a single reliable data point to back it up.

Not because the data doesn't exist. It does — in spreadsheets, in Tally, in WhatsApp messages, in someone's head. But it's scattered, inconsistent, and nobody has ever connected it into something useful.

The data was never the problem

Ask most SME owners whether they track their numbers and the answer is almost always yes. Sales get logged. Inventory gets counted. Expenses get recorded. The problem isn't a lack of tracking — it's that the tracking lives in five different places, in five different formats, updated on five different schedules, with nobody responsible for reconciling them into one version of the truth.

By the time a decision needs to get made, someone is manually stitching together numbers from three spreadsheets and a gut feeling. That's not a data problem. That's a systems problem wearing a data costume.

Why this gap is about to matter more

The businesses that will separate themselves over the next decade aren't necessarily the ones with the biggest budgets or the best products. They'll be the ones that figured out how to run tighter — tracking the right numbers, spotting problems early, and making decisions based on evidence instead of instinct.

Data analytics isn't a luxury for large enterprises anymore. For Indian SMEs, it's becoming a competitive necessity.

The gap between businesses that act on this and businesses that don't is widening every year — not because the ones acting on it are smarter, but because they can see problems three months before the ones who are still reconciling spreadsheets on a Friday night.

Where this actually starts

It doesn't start with a dashboard. It starts with agreeing where the single source of truth lives, cleaning up how data enters that system in the first place, and only then building the reporting layer on top. Skip that order and you've just built a better-looking version of the same mess.
