There's No One-Size-Fits-All ERP

NORTHSTAR TEAM · ERP CUSTOMIZATION

ERP software doesn't come with a one-size-fits-all setting.

What works for a 500-person manufacturing company will break a 20-person trading business trying to use the same setup. Every business has its own workflows, its own terminology, its own way of moving information between people. An ERP that isn't configured around those realities doesn't get adopted — it gets worked around.

What "worked around" actually looks like

It rarely looks dramatic. Teams find small shortcuts first — one field left blank because it doesn't apply to how they actually work, one approval step skipped because it adds a day for no reason. Then parallel spreadsheets appear, quietly, to hold the information the system was never configured to capture properly. Within a few months, the WhatsApp group has become the real system of record, and the ERP has become the thing people update after the fact, to keep management happy.

None of that is a software failure. It's a configuration failure — the system was mapped to a template, not to the business.

Understanding the business comes first

The value of ERP consulting isn't in knowing the software. Plenty of people know the software. It's in understanding the business first — its processes, its people, its growth plans — and then mapping the software to fit that reality, not the other way around.

Customisation isn't complexity for its own sake. It's the difference between a system that works and a system that sits there.

What good customization actually means

Good customization isn't about adding every possible feature — that's how you end up with the over-engineered system nobody can operate. It's about a small number of deliberate decisions: which approvals actually need a human, which fields matter to this specific business, which reports this specific leadership team will actually open. Get those right and the system disappears into the way people already work. Get them wrong, and you've built an expensive piece of shelf-ware with a login screen.
